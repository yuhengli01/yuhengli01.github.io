Can Reinforcement Learning Solve Asymmetric Combinatorial-Continuous Zero-Sum Games?
Yuheng Li, Panpan Wang, Haipeng Chen
ICLR 2025 (Poster)
arxiv code poster slides
Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning Game Theory
Abstract

Existing work on learning in zero-sum games largely assumes symmetric or matrix-form strategy spaces. We define and study ACCES games — two-player zero-sum games in which one player chooses from a combinatorial action space and the other from an infinite, continuous one — a structure that arises naturally in combinatorial optimization under adversarial or uncertain parameters (e.g., routing, covering, and patrolling). We prove a mixed Nash equilibrium exists via essentially-finite-game approximation, then propose CCDO, a combinatorial–continuous double-oracle solver with convergence guarantees, and its practical variant CCDO-RL, which uses reinforcement learning to compute approximate best responses. On adversarial covering-salesman, capacitated-routing, and patrolling games, CCDO-RL converges to approximate equilibria and generalizes to unseen instances.

LENS: Learning to Navigate with Active Search for Partially Observable MAPF in Unknown Environments
Di Yang, Yuheng Li, Yanhai Xiong
Transactions on Machine Learning Research (TMLR)
paper openreview
Diffusion Model Multi-Agent Path Finding
Abstract

LENS tackles centralized, partially observable multi-agent path finding (PO-MAPF), where a team must navigate and coordinate in an environment it discovers as it moves. Rather than one-step reactive policies or global-search-with-local-repair, LENS runs search locally over anticipated conflict groups and learns to regress a continuous potential field — a dense prediction (a U-Net proposer with FiLM guidance) that captures longer-horizon structure and helps agents recognize and pass through bottlenecks. A conflict-resolution cascade (L-CBS, coordinated retreat, push-and-rotate) then guarantees locally collision-free motion within bounded spatio-temporal windows, without large-scale end-to-end policy learning.
